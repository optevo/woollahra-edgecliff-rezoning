# Submission on the Edgecliff-Woollahra State-led Rezoning

**To:** Department of Planning, Housing and Infrastructure (the Department), Edgecliff-Woollahra Precinct State-led Rezoning

**Position:** I object to the proposal as exhibited. I ask that it be fixed and re-exhibited with the missing analysis and the draft instrument (section 2).

## How to read this submission

Page numbers are PDF page numbers; printed page numbers differ in several of the exhibited documents. Short forms (EIE, UDR and so on) are defined in "Documents cited" at the end. Paragraphs are numbered by section so that the Department, Council and others can refer to them exactly.

Three kinds of statement are kept apart throughout. What the Department's documents say is quoted and footnoted. What I have calculated, from those documents or from public data, is labelled "my estimate", with its basis in a sentence and a range. What I have seen as a resident is labelled as mine ("In my experience", "I observed") and is not footnoted as if it were documentary. The calculations behind my estimates are in the appendix, with the public sources they read.

## Summary

**S.1** I am a Paddington resident who knows this area well. I lived opposite 400 Glenmore Road until about three years ago and I still live nearby. I support more housing at Edgecliff, and I accept that new supply helps. My objection is to how the proposal gets from a number to a rule, and to what it leaves out.

**S.2** **What this submission is.** It is a resident's test of whether the right questions have been asked. I have not tried to do the Department's work. Where I give an estimate, I use the Department's own documents and charts or the State's own parameters, I say how the estimate was made, how far it moves, and which of the Department's data would settle it. I accept the limits of that approach. My claim is that the results are large enough, and the gaps clear enough, to warrant proper investigation. The common thread is structural: the exhibition is a capacity exercise, not a delivery plan, and the links between the number and what is built are missing (section 2).

**S.3** **The plan is sized to a number set at announcement, and that number has not been tested at what the Department itself expects.** The Department's documents give 9,400 homes. Its own economist expects about 4,800 by 2046, about half, and about 2,400 of those would be built under the existing controls anyway.[^1] Costs and impacts are studied at the full number. Apart from the economic assessment's count of benefits and the Strategy's count of contributions (144 at 3% of 4,800), I found no exhibited study run at or below the 4,800; the lowest transport case is 6,000 (section 3).

**S.4** **Services lag the population.** At full build the plan roughly halves open space per resident, from about 13.9 to 6.8 m² (my arithmetic), into the "limited" band of the Department's own mapping,[^2] and the Infrastructure Delivery Plan's works schedule lists no new school.[^3] The assurances on schools and utilities rest on advice that is not exhibited (section 8).

**S.5** **The permission arrives in 2027 and the support arrives later, with nothing linking them.** The station's completion is marked "subject to change", the stormwater works the flood result depends on are listed as ten or more years away, and the transport plan says its initiatives are "not tied to specific development thresholds or sequencing triggers".[^4] The controls are due by the end of 2027 (section 4).

**S.6** **The Department expects partial delivery but has not tested it.** Its answer for a site that does not assemble is that "existing planning controls will remain in effect".[^5] The Explanation removes the Low and Mid-Rise provisions from rezoned sites, so that is below the economic assessment's own Base Case, and no study is run on a precinct in which some key sites build and others do not. On the Department's own feasibility charts the margin over its lower land-cost line is thin, and within 200 m of Edgecliff Station at 3.0:1 it is below that line. I do not offer a competing forecast. My test is narrower: the State's own take-up rate gives the Department's 4,800 only if every one of the 9,400 homes is on a feasible site and none needs assembling, and the Department's own documents say that most sites need two to five lots assembled and that its charts show thin margins. If either is right, 4,800 is the top of the range, not the middle. My estimate is that about 6% to 41% of key-site capacity reaches a development application by 2046, central about 18%, and my judgement after heritage, strata ownership and slope is about 600 to 2,300 homes (section 5).

**S.7** **Affordable housing: against a baseline of no redevelopment, my estimate is that the contribution is smaller than the low-rent stock removed, and nothing exhibited shows otherwise.** The exhibition has no baseline of the low-rent homes it removes. It leaves open how a contributed dwelling is kept at an affordable rent and how long, allows cash payment, has no replacement duty that I can find, and shows a nil rate on three key sites that, on my matching, include older strata flats. My estimate at the moderate-income rent is a net change in the precinct of about −190 to −20 affordable dwellings at my central build-out and about −610 to −80 at the Department's own 4,800; at the low-income rent the estimate straddles zero and I draw no conclusion. Across Sydney the answer depends on whether the cash is spent promptly on housing (section 6).

**S.8** **One site I know well, 400 Glenmore Road, shows how these gaps meet.** On the Department's maps, Council's flood advice and my own estimates (labelled in section 7), it combines a tower about 5.5 times the current height limit inside the Paddington Heritage Conservation Area, a floodway and high-hazard flood setting, a ground and basement question, an overshadowing effect on the 120 homes opposite that needs checking, and a junction that has not been assessed for the combined load. I ask that it be removed; failing that, that the existing controls be retained or only modestly increased if the assessments support it; failing that, that no height be approved until the named assessments are exhibited (section 7).

**S.9** **Process.** Ten items the exhibited documents rely on are not exhibited, including the draft instrument. Section 9 sets out these matters factually, without drawing legal conclusions, together with the obligations I understand bear on them, and asks the Department to record its response in its Finalisation Report.

**S.10** **What I ask.** That the Department complete the missing analysis, publish it with the draft instrument, and re-exhibit a revised, staged proposal before the controls are made. If it proceeds, I ask for staged release tied to funded delivery starting with Edgecliff, the affordable-housing rent rule, replacement and cash rules written into the instrument, services before occupation of towers, removal or deferral of the Trumper Park sites pending the flood, junction and heritage analysis, and annual monitoring with a re-exhibition trigger. At a minimum, I ask for the baseline and the key-site feasibility results to be published. The table below sets out the requests; the full schedule is at the end.

## 1. Who I am and why I am writing

**1.1** I live in Paddington. Until about three years ago I lived close to 400 Glenmore Road, the lot that appears to be key site TR.1.1 in this proposal. I still live nearby, and the junction of Glenmore Road and New South Head Road is the main way I leave Paddington for the city, the airport and the west. I say openly that I am a resident of the area. I raise the site because I know it. I declare that I own a property in Paddington, outside the precinct but within easy walking distance of it; I make no claim about its value or about any effect on it.

**1.2** In my experience the streets this proposal loads are narrower and steeper than a map suggests. I drive between Paddington and Double Bay most weeks. Wallaroy Road runs steeply down from near the roundabout, and with cars parked on both sides it works as a single lane. I have experienced congestion on Forth Street and on Queen Street around school finishing time and through the evening peak, from about 3 pm onwards, and Forth Street is particularly difficult because of the school. I have no dated record of these observations; they are what I regularly experience.

**1.3** I am president of a local school-age care service, and I am responsible for hiring its senior staff. In my experience our senior staff cannot afford to live near the school, so they commute long distances, which costs them and adds to traffic. That is one organisation, so it is an illustration and not a measurement. I do not name the organisation or any staff member.

**1.4** I am not opposed to growth. Edgecliff is already a working transport hub, and adding housing there makes sense. I take no position on the station itself; if it proceeds, that area could also carry more housing. There is a case for reasonable development here. My objection is to a proposal that, on the Department's own documents, has not shown that its scale can be delivered, secured or supported.

**1.5** I have lived in several historic European cities, and I place a high value on heritage in the places where people live. Woollahra and Paddington are, in my view, among the best examples in Sydney of the "missing middle": walkable, tree-covered, with heritage buildings that give the area the feel of a village. I raise this as a resident's view, not as a finding; the heritage evidence is in sections 5, 7 and 8.

**1.6** I am not aware of any consultation with the community before the announcement of the station and the housing target on 24 August 2025. Council's submission says that, following the announcement, the Department engaged a consultant team, and that "since late 2025" key Council staff have engaged with the Department and that team through regular working meetings.[^6] I do not suggest bad faith. I say only that it has cost trust, and that a thorough and open response to submissions is the best way to recover some of it.

## 2. The objection in brief, and what I ask

**2.1** **The objection in one paragraph.** The proposal is sized to a headline number rather than to what can be delivered, secured and supported. The documents give 9,400 homes; the Department's economist expects about half of that by 2046; and, apart from the economic assessment's count of benefits and the Strategy's count of contributions, I found no study run at or below that half, or that tests where the missing half would sit. The permission is given in 2027 and the support (station, stormwater, utilities, schools, local transport, affordable housing) arrives later, unfunded or unpublished, with no trigger linking the two. The Department's own documents expect partial delivery but do not test it. The result is a plan whose benefits depend on sites whose assembly is uncertain, whose affordable housing is not shown to be secured against the low-rent homes it removes, and whose edges (the Paddington conservation area, 400 Glenmore Road) are not assessed.

**2.2** **The structural problem.** The exhibition is a capacity exercise, not a delivery plan. Five links are missing between the number and what happens on the ground: (a) a case for what will actually be built, where and when, as distinct from what the controls allow; (b) a link between the permission and the support it relies on; (c) a tested fallback for the partial delivery the Department itself expects; (d) a baseline of what the plan removes, and a test of the net effect; and (e) monitoring, with a point at which the plan is revisited. Sections 3 to 6 take these in turn; section 7 shows them meeting on one site; sections 8 and 9 cover what follows; section 10 describes a version that closes the links. These are gaps in the analysis that the Department can fill. I do not say the proposal cannot be made to work.

**2.3** **What I concede.** Edgecliff is an existing hub and could take more housing. New supply helps over time and across the region, and I do not argue otherwise (section 6 gives the size of the effect). The Department is candid in places: its economist says the full figure is a "theoretical capacity",[^7] its Strategy says "not all land will be developed",[^8] and its Flooding report declines to upzone where depths exceed one to two metres.[^9] Its construction cost inputs are not obviously low (section 5). Listed heritage items are retained in several places, and the proposal includes new parks and links. My points are about what is missing, not about the Department's good faith.

**2.4** **Anticipating the Department's response: urgency.** The Department may say that the housing shortage is urgent and that gaps can be closed later. I accept the urgency. But as I understand it, permission is capitalised into land prices as soon as it is announced and cannot be recalled; benefits that depend on assembly cannot be repaired by a later amendment once owners have priced the uplift; and the analyses I ask for (a baseline, key-site feasibility, staging) are within the Department's reach and much cheaper to do before the controls are made than to correct afterwards. Urgency is a reason to fix the gaps promptly, not to skip them.

**2.5** **Primary request: fix and re-exhibit.** I ask that the Department not finalise the controls as exhibited. It should (a) complete the analysis the exhibition does not contain: the studies at the take-up the Department expects, the key-site feasibility results, a baseline of the housing the plan removes, interim-state flood and junction modelling, and shadow analysis for existing homes; (b) publish that analysis with the draft instrument; and (c) re-exhibit a revised, staged proposal before the controls are made. I put this first because several of the gaps would change the proposal, not only its description.

**2.6** **Fallback if the Department proceeds.** If it proceeds with the controls substantially as exhibited, I ask for:

- staged release of capacity, tied to funded delivery and starting with Edgecliff, gated on completions rather than approvals (section 10);
- how a contributed dwelling is kept affordable, the base of the percentage, one-for-one replacement of low-rent homes, and the cash rules written into the instrument, and no nil rate on sites that remove older low-rent flats;
- services before occupation of towers;
- removal or deferral of the Trumper Park sites, including 400 Glenmore Road, pending the flood, junction and heritage analysis; and
- annual monitoring, with a re-exhibition trigger.

**2.7** **Minimum.** Whatever else the Department decides, I ask that it publish the baseline of existing homes and the key-site feasibility results. The first is the basis of any claim about net affordable housing; the second is the basis of any claim about what will be built.

**2.8** **What would make re-exhibition necessary.** In my view, re-exhibition would be warranted if the response involved a materially different affordable-housing scheme, a new staging arrangement, or changes to the key sites or their heights, because any of those would change what submitters have been asked to comment on.

**2.9** **Scope and limits.** This is a resident's analysis of public documents and public data, made without the data the Department holds. It is not a substitute for the Department's own analysis and does not claim to be. It is offered to show that the questions are worth asking: each estimate uses the Department's own inputs or the State's own parameters, says how it was made and how far it moves, and names the data that would settle it. My estimates are indicative and I give them as ranges. Where I have made a calculation I say so, state its basis, and set out the code in the appendix so that it can be checked and re-run. If my numbers are wrong, the way to show it is to publish the Department's, and I would welcome that.

**2.10** **What I have not been able to assess.** The main things a resident cannot assess without the Department's data, and why each matters, are:

- the number, ownership, strata status and site areas of the lots in each key site, which decide whether a site can assemble;
- the existing tenure, rents and tenants of the homes on the change sites, which decide whether the plan removes affordable housing;
- the Department's feasibility results for each key site, which decide what is likely to be built;
- the tower form, position and floor plate on 400 Glenmore Road, which decide the shadow and flood effects there;
- the current performance of the Glenmore Road and New South Head Road junction, which decides whether it can take any added load; and
- the advice of the Department of Education, Sydney Water and Ausgrid, which decides whether the services assumed will be there.

**2.11** **Method and Council.** My analysis used public data, code and an AI research assistant; the core code is in the appendix. I have checked each quotation and page reference to the exhibited documents and to Council's submission against the published PDFs. Sources I could not open are cited as reported. Where my points agree with Council's public submission I cite it as support, without implying any coordination, and I treat Council's statements as Council's. I have not seen the reports by Council's consultants that are not in Council's submission.

## 3. A number set at announcement, and tested afterwards

**3.1** **What the documents say about the number.** The headline is not one quantity. The documents use five:

| Quantity | Value | Where |
|----------|------:|-------|
| Announcement and "target" | up to 10,000 | 24 August 2025 release; "the target of 10,000 additional dwellings"[^10] |
| Capacity in the controls | about 9,400 | EIE p6; the Urban Design Report's brief is "up to 9,400 homes"[^11] |
| Theoretical capacity | 9,000 to 10,000 | Economic Impact Assessment[^7] |
| Expected delivery by 2046 (Proposal Case) | about 4,800, "approximately half" | Economic Impact Assessment[^12] |
| Delivery under existing controls (Base Case) | about 2,400 | Economic Impact Assessment[^13] |

The difference between the last two, about 2,400, is the increment the rezoning is expected to add (my arithmetic). It is about a quarter of 9,400.

**3.2** **How the number arose.** I do not allege a purpose; I describe what the documents say. The Explanation says the proposal "has been drafted to align with" the station announcement.[^14] The Department's Deputy Secretary told Budget Estimates on 25 August 2025 that the figure came from modelling of lot size, distance to the station and "some preliminary built form controls, just to get a sense of what might be an estimate of homes", and that detailed master planning was still to come.[^15] That shows how the figure arose. It does not show that the studies later tested it.

**3.3** **Where the number is explained, the explanation is missing.** The State Design Review Panel asked for "a defensible rationale for dwelling yield". The reply describes a method and shows no derivation, and the "six scenarios" the Urban Design Report says were tested appear nowhere in it.[^16] The FAQ says feasibility testing was used to "determine appropriate housing numbers, building heights, site amalgamations, affordable housing contributions and supporting infrastructure".[^17] The feasibility results shown in the Affordable Housing Strategy are for typologies up to 4.0:1 residential and 6.0:1 mixed use. The key sites, up to 9.0:1, are described as tested and no result is shown.[^18]

**3.4** **No comparison with alternatives.** I searched the Explanation, the FAQ and the Urban Design Report and found no comparison of the proposal with alternative outcomes. Council's Alternative Approach proposes accommodating about 2,200 to 3,600 additional dwellings through a targeted, place-based strategy.[^19] I express no view on whether that is the right number, but the exhibition does not say why the Department's capacity was preferred to it, and the "six scenarios" it refers to are not shown.

**3.5** **How each study is sized.** The studies I have read take the following bases:

| Study | Sized to | At about half take-up |
|-------|----------|-----------------------|
| Transport (TMAP) | 6,000, 8,000 and 10,000 homes; the operational model was run once, for 2046 at the full yield[^20] | None at or below about 5,000 and none without the station. The TMAP itself calls 10,000 by 2046 "unlikely"[^21] |
| Infrastructure Delivery Plan | 10,000 homes, $1.3 billion; excludes land acquisition and station opening cost[^22] | The developer share ($556m, 41.7% by my arithmetic) and 17 developer-delivered public-benefit items depend on take-up[^23] |
| Utilities | 10,000 apartments, 38.08 MVA[^24] | Staging is "flexible" (p 9) but the timing of electrical upgrades is "difficult to predict without a clear forecast uptake" (p 23)[^25] |
| Flooding | Full redevelopment, amalgamated lots, all upgrades built[^26] | Not run at partial take-up |
| Social and community | 9,350 homes and 35,385 residents[^27] | About 10,000 new residents by 2046 at the Department's own take-up (my arithmetic) |
| Visual, wind, thermal | Maximum envelopes, "worst-case"[^28] | Not modelled as a patchwork |

**3.6** **The pattern.** Costs and impacts are loaded at the full number. The Economic Impact Assessment's headline benefits ($2.8 billion of output and 6,068 jobs) are reported net of the Base Case, so they rest on the increment of about 2,400 homes and not on 4,800.[^29] My reading, which the Department can correct, is that costs and impacts are sized to about 100% of the capacity, the benefits to about half, and the increment attributable to the rezoning to about a quarter.

**3.7** **Three baselines that do not agree.** The Economic Impact Assessment's Base Case assumes about 2,400 homes under existing controls.[^30] The transport plan's 2046 base adds about 270 dwellings.[^31] The Explanation's fallback removes the Low and Mid-Rise provisions on rezoned sites, so a key site that does not earn the incentive may fall below the Economic Impact Assessment's own Base Case.[^32] Each study answers a different question, and none says so.

**3.8** **The delivery pace implied.** The Economic Impact Assessment expects about 240 homes a year (my arithmetic from its 4,800 over 20 years). It compares this with 120 a year at Chatswood, 140 at Bondi Junction and 560 at Epping.[^30] The pace is plausible only if the sites assemble.

**3.9** **Strategic context.** The Urban Design Report says Council's local strategic planning statement was prepared in the context of projected growth of 2,050 residents ("circa 1,000 dwellings") over 20 years across the whole local government area, and that the area has no identified major growth corridors.[^33] The 9,400 is about 9.4 times that (my arithmetic). The Department will say that statement dates from 2020 and that the Sydney Plan now recognises the precinct, and I accept that.[^34]

**3.10** **Anticipating the Department's response.** First, that capacity is not a mandate and 4,800 is the forecast. I agree, and that is my point: the studies should then be shown at the forecast and at a low case, and the benefits should be tied to what is delivered. Secondly, that sizing to the full capacity is prudent. For traffic that may be so, because fewer homes means fewer trips, and I say so in section 5. It is not prudent for funding, flood, utilities or the benefits, which fall with take-up while the lumpy items (a station entrance, a trunk main, a substation, flood storage) do not.

**3.11** **What I ask.** Requests R3.1 to R3.5 in the schedule.

## 4. Permission now, support later

**4.1** **The controls come first.** The Explanation says the proposal is "expected to be finalised by the end of 2027".[^35] I found no exhibited clause that releases capacity on delivery of the station, drainage, schools or anything else. The transport plan says its initiatives are "not tied to specific development thresholds or sequencing triggers" and describes its staging as "without clear triggers or staging beyond the delivery of the new Woollahra Station".[^36] The Infrastructure Delivery Plan promises a staging framework with "indicative sequencing and cashflow considerations" and then gives five generic next steps.[^37]

**4.2** **The support comes later.** All thirteen stormwater items are listed as "Following Development / Long Term (10+ years)" and funded from "Council / Local Contributions".[^38] The Flooding report's favourable results assume that every upgrade, "both essential and non-essential", is built, and I found no interim or essential-only run. The report separates "essential" upgrades required to support the rezoning from "non-essential but preferable" ones, and says staging may need interim measures, but it did not model the split.[^39] The utilities report says commitment "must commence well in advance of development delivery" and that "all advice remains preliminary".[^40]

**4.3** **What the Plan does time early.** The Plan times the ten parks and the station (item PT5) in the first five years, but the parks are developer-delivered through key sites and so depend on assembly (section 5), and the drainage on which the flood result depends is timed after development and ten or more years away.[^41]

**4.4** **The station.** I do not say the station is unfunded. The Government has said construction is expected to start in 2027 and finish in 2029, subject to planning approval (release of 9 January 2026).[^42] But the exhibited documents give no station cost figure or funding commitment. The Infrastructure Delivery Plan lists the station (item PT5) as "State Funding" with a cost band of "$$$" and no figure, excludes any "station opening cost" from its total, and says the Transport Project Component covers "part of the cost"; the Explanation calls that contribution "proposed".[^43] The station's completion is marked "subject to change".[^44] The Cabinet Office's briefing gives a cost range of $147m to $186m.[^45] The station is real. What is missing is any link between it, or the other supports, and the permission.

**4.5** **Who depends on the station.** About two-thirds of the uplift, about 6,400 of the 9,400 homes, is in the character areas the Department places in the Woollahra Station catchment (my grouping of the Urban Design Report's area totals; not a measured 800 m line).[^46] Nobody has tested the plan without the station, with it late, or in an interim year. The Economic Impact Assessment concedes only that "a shift to these timeframes would result in a shift in estimated economic activity".[^30] The transport plan's three growth scenarios include the station and its base case does not, so the comparison it plots mixes the effect of the station with the effect of the housing.[^47]

**4.6** **The funding depends on take-up.** The Plan assumes a 4% local levy; the plan in force authorises 1%, and the Infrastructure Delivery Plan itself records the risk that an increase may not align with the rezoning timeline.[^48] Council says it may need to seek more than 1%.[^49] The Plan's Council delivery-responsibility line is $314.45m.[^50] The Transport Project Component is $15,000 a dwelling within 800 m of the station.[^51] My estimate is that at my central take-up (section 5) that component raises about $12m to $16m, which is 6% to 11% of the $147m to $186m station cost range; at full build it would raise about $96m (6,400 homes at $15,000), which is 52% to 65%. The station is meant to be partly paid for by housing that, on the Department's own parameters, arrives at a fraction of the assumed rate.

**4.7** **What I ask for instead.** The proposal already uses a gate at site level: the incentive controls "will only apply where the consent authority is satisfied that the relevant key sites requirements have been achieved".[^52] I ask that the same device be applied at precinct scale. Capacity would be released in tranches, each tied to dated, funded delivery of the station and of the drainage, transport, school and utility works the studies depend on (section 10 sets out a possible structure). At the Economic Impact Assessment's own pace of about 240 homes a year, a first tranche of about 2,400 homes would last about ten years (my arithmetic), so the gate costs little if the Department's own account is right.

**4.8** **Anticipating the Department's response.** That the station is real and works have begun; that housing arrives slowly anyway; and that the Government is committed. I accept the first. If housing does arrive slowly, a trigger costs little. If it does not, the trigger is what protects the people who live here. The Department may also say that infrastructure staging is intended. It is: the Infrastructure Delivery Plan says so. What is absent is any staging of the release of development capacity, and I found none in the Explanation, FAQ, Maps, Design Guide, Urban Design Report or Infrastructure Delivery Plan.

**4.9** **What I ask.** Requests R4.1 to R4.5 in the schedule.

## 5. If only part is built

**5.1** **The Department expects partial delivery and has not tested it.** Its Strategy says "not all land will be developed",[^8] its economic assessment says renewal areas "rarely realise their full theoretical development capacity",[^53] and its Urban Design Report says "most sites require multi-lot amalgamation", typically two to five lots.[^54] The answer for a site that does not assemble is that "existing planning controls will remain in effect", and the draft "does not consider a lower development outcome without amalgamation".[^5] The Infrastructure Delivery Plan adds that infrastructure across fragmented sites will come "through progressive land dedication via incentive planning controls";[^55] I found no clause or study showing how that works when the first lot builds and its neighbours do not. The Explanation removes the Low and Mid-Rise provisions from rezoned sites, so the controls that continue are below the economic assessment's own Base Case.[^32] The incentive applies only where key-site requirements are "achieved", a word the Explanation does not define, and they "may be refined".[^52] The key-site table has no amalgamation column, so I found no count of the sites, or of the 9,400 homes, that depend on assembly, although the Urban Design Report's key-site map colours "Proposed Amalgamation Sites" without counting them.[^56] By "fragmentation" I mean that some key sites assemble and build and others do not; the studies assess only the fully built state.

**5.7** **The Department's inputs are not obviously low.** Its construction range brackets the Rider Levett Bucknall figures for Sydney, and its basement cost of $120,000 a space is above the independent range of about $55,000 to $100,000.[^63] I do not rely on a claim that its costs are too low. My point is that on its own inputs the margin is thin, and that I found no tower price for Woollahra, Edgecliff or Paddington and no figure for deep sandstone excavation.[^64]

### The towers

**5.8** The Department's cost schedule rises with height, from $4,500 to $6,500 per square metre of building area.[^65] At a floor space ratio near 3:1, a 30-storey form therefore earns no more floor space than a 10-storey form on the same site and costs more per square metre (my reading of the schedule). To rank the 156 key sites I applied my rebuild to each site's floor space ratio and height. This is a screen, not a feasibility result: it uses one generic land-cost band ($20,000 and $25,000 as the lines), where the Department's charts use several from $15,000 to $40,000; it interpolates the cost range linearly between 4 and 30 storeys; and it has no site's existing-use value, which is unpublished. On this screen 54 sites are not feasible, 66 marginal and 36 feasible; of the 36 towers, 12 are in each class; eight of the ten Cliff Edge North sites are not feasible. Moving the thresholds by $3,000 either way gives between 30, 56 and 70 and 95, 47 and 14, and the Department's lower Woollahra bands move "not feasible" from 54 to 28. It will be wrong for individual sites in both directions. I offer it as an order of magnitude and a reason to ask which sites the Department's own model finds feasible (R5.1). Treasury modelling reported in the press finds a 26-storey apartment building unfeasible (a margin one point below the hurdle) where a 16-storey one is feasible (12 points above); I have not read the primary report.[^66]

### How much is likely to be built

- **Share of key-site capacity reaching a development application by 2046:** about 6% to 41% across the amalgamation penalty and the threshold choice, central about 18% (about 1,730 homes; range about 540 to 3,900).
- **Over 40 years,** the central case rises to about 32%, and the no-penalty case at my base thresholds reaches about 52%.
- **As a cross-check on the State's 4% a year:** (1) if all 9,400 homes were feasible and no site needed assembly, 20 years at 4% a year gives about 5,250, almost exactly the Department's 4,800; (2) if 60% were feasible and none needed assembly, about 3,150; (3) if all were feasible and the State's "five times" penalty applied to every site, about 1,400.

A development application is not a completion, so these are upper bounds. I apply the amalgamation penalty to every site because the exhibition does not say which need assembling; the central 0.5 is my judgement between no penalty and the State's upper bound, and mapping my three feasibility classes onto the State's categories is an assumption. I do not offer this as a competing forecast. My test is narrower: the Department's 4,800 is what the State's own take-up rate gives only if every one of the 9,400 homes is on a feasible site and none needs assembling. The Department's charts show most Edgecliff cases at or near the lower land-cost line, and its Urban Design Report says most sites need two to five lots assembled. If either is right, 4,800 is the top of the range, not its middle; only the Department's feasibility results can settle it. ### What depends on assembly

**5.11** The benefits are attached to the key sites. A park on a stalled site does not arrive, and the Infrastructure Delivery Plan excludes land acquisition, so there is no fallback owner.[^69] A through-site link stops at the first unassembled lot, the "isolated outcomes" the Urban Design Report says it wants to avoid.[^70] The flood corridors, about 1,060 m on private land (my sum of the corridor lengths in the Design Guide[^71]), are assumed in the flood result to be cleared and the lots amalgamated.[^72] The second Woollahra Station entrance is "a key site requirement" for the adjoining land.[^73]

**5.12** A link or corridor needs several sites to build together. Council reports one link that depends on three key sites (24 properties, 74 or more landowners, nine strata schemes) and a landscape loop that depends on eight (132 landowners); these are Council's figures and the underlying data is not exhibited.[^74] If each site builds independently with my central probability of 14%, the chance that all three on the link build is 0.3% (14% cubed). If one developer assembles a whole block, the chain's chance is nearer 14%. The true figure lies between, and for a chain that crosses owners it is nearer the lower bound.

### Towers before their services

**5.15** Only 17 of the 36 towers carry a required minimum of retail or commercial floor space, and ten, at Cliff Edge South, Edgecliff and Woollahra Centre, carry no retail, open space or community facility on their own site.[^57] At my central take-up the precinct gains about 2,800 to 3,700 new residents by 2046 (my arithmetic, at about 2.1 people a home from the Department's own ratio[^27]), mostly in six or seven towers among unbuilt neighbours. The Social and Community Infrastructure Assessment computes demand at full build only.

**5.16** Experience elsewhere is that the gap tends to close years later, not never. At Upper Strathfield, AHURI found ground-floor units vacant or used as offices, no local shops, and harm to all residents "irrespective of the contributions the developers of their buildings made" (a single developer owned most of the properties).[^75] The Department's own Sydney Plan says large open spaces should be secured "before development progresses"; the exhibition has no such trigger.[^76] The neighbouring centres reduce the risk at Edgecliff but not at Cliff Edge, the Woollahra Station block or Trumper Park.

**5.17** **The heritage interface.** The plan puts a 16-storey tower on an amalgamated site at 67 to 71 Ocean Street and 2 Wellington Street. The consultant's text assumes the tower is set back behind the principal building lines of the neighbouring heritage items I529, I530 and I651, as "a key interface control", so that they are "not visually dominated by the rear tower form".[^77] If that site assembles and its neighbours do not, the tower stands beside them for as long as they remain, and I found no assessment of that interim state. The consultant's support is conditional on staging, concentrating yield and amalgamating only non-contributory sites, which the exhibition does not adopt.[^78]

**5.18** **Where partial delivery is better.** Fewer homes at any one time means less traffic, wind, noise and construction at any one time, and fewer demolitions. I accept that, and the transport plan's own statement that 10,000 by 2046 is "unlikely" supports it.[^21] The objection is that the plan does not say a slower build-out is acceptable, does not secure it, and does not test it.

**5.19** **Anticipating the Department's response.** First, that assembly is voluntary, so nothing is lost if it fails. The loss is not the owner's: it is the benefits attached to the lot, the interface effects on neighbours, and the studies sized to a build-out that does not occur. Secondly, that owners assemble at a premium. A consultant's study for Strathfield Council reports premiums of up to 50% over existing-use value, with 20% "more common",[^79] against headroom of 7% to 24% in the Department's own cases; no premium or holdout rate is published. Thirdly, that strata law provides a pathway. It does, but a collective sale is per scheme and goes to the Land and Environment Court, and the State's policy says amalgamation makes development far less likely.[^80]

**5.20** **What I ask.** Requests R5.1 to R5.6 in the schedule.

## 6. Affordable housing

> **Key findings, written to be quoted whole.** Figures marked "my estimate" are indicative ranges from public data. They are offered to show that the question needs answering; the Department holds the records that would settle it.
>
> **1. There is no baseline.** No exhibited document counts the existing low-rent homes, tenants or rents on the change sites, or tests for a net loss. The implied number of existing homes removed is 2,862 (the Strategy's 12,262 gross capacity less its 9,400 net, my arithmetic), which agrees with Council's 2,860 homes on 653 properties.[^81]
>
> **2. The contribution is small, and the exhibition leaves open how it is kept affordable.** The base rate of 3%, nil on some key sites, is about 3 to 5.5 affordable homes per 100 new homes (central about 3.4), depending on what the 3% is a percentage of. The Strategy says contributions pass to Council as an "outright contribution" and the Explanation says a community housing provider must manage the dwellings; that is a stronger footing than the 15-year in-fill bonus. The exhibition does not say how a dwelling held by Council is kept at an affordable rent, what "a reasonable time" and "an adjoining area" mean for cash, or what happens when a provider's term ends.[^82]
>
> **3. No replacement duty is exhibited.** As I understand it (I could not obtain the clause), the Housing SEPP retention levy does not apply to a building already approved for strata subdivision, and many older flats are strata-titled. The Explanation switches off only the in-fill bonus, and the Proposal attaches no net-loss test to the key sites.[^83]
>
> **4. Against a baseline of no redevelopment, my estimate is that the contribution is smaller than the low-rent stock removed.** About 130 rented homes among those removed (Monte Carlo range 62 to 356) are affordable at the low-income rent, and about 450 (229 to 860) at the moderate-income rent. At my central build-out of about 1,350 homes the net change in the precinct by 2046 is about −77 (−192 to −17) affordable dwellings at the moderate-income test; at the Department's own 4,800 homes it is about −287 (−606 to −83). At the low-income test the estimate straddles zero and I draw no conclusion. The Base Case would also remove some of this stock without a mandatory contribution, so the increment attributable to the proposal may differ; that needs the Department's register.
>
> **5. Across Sydney the answer depends on the cash.** If the cash is spent promptly on homes at about 1.5 dwellings per dwelling-equivalent, the Sydney-wide net at the low-income test is roughly neutral to positive, and at the moderate-income test it is negative in about three draws in four. If the cash is spent late, elsewhere or for few homes, the net is close to minus the homes lost. The statute and the Explanation give "the area or an adjoining area", "a reasonable time" and Government-owned land in this or adjacent LGAs; none is a deadline or a place inside the precinct.
>
> **6. The mechanism matters more than the rate.** In my modelling a 12% scheme paid 90% in cash is still a net loss at the moderate-income test. One-for-one replacement of low-rent homes removes the loss by construction, which is why I ask for it.

**6.1** **What the exhibition says.** The Explanation says the base rate is 3% on "all new residential development within the Precinct", up to 15% on mapped sites, satisfied "either through floor space/units or equivalent monetary contribution".[^84] The Economic Impact Assessment says the rezoning seeks to balance housing diversity and affordable housing "by facilitating contributions in monetary form".[^85] Three key sites (2-4 New McLean Street, 62 Ocean Street and 2-8 Weeroona Avenue) carry nil because of "other planning obligation/s and material public benefit".[^86] The Strategy counts the contribution as dwellings ("at a minimum there would be contributions equivalent to 144 affordable housing apartments (at a base rate of 3%)", which is 3% of 4,800, my arithmetic) but tests it as "sales revenue foregone".[^87] The in-fill bonus is switched off in the precinct and the Low and Mid-Rise provisions are removed on rezoned sites.[^88]

**6.2** **Duration and cash.** The Strategy says the State scheme is "different to" the in-fill scheme (affordable for at least 15 years) and the transport-oriented scheme ("in-perpetuity (forever)"), and that contributions "take the form of a monetary payment (cash) or land or dwellings and ownership passes to the council".[^89] I accept that this is a stronger footing than the 15-year bonus. For cash, the statutory duty it quotes is to apply the money for affordable housing "in the area or an adjoining area within a reasonable time".[^90] The Department's consultant says dwellings scattered in luxury buildings "are not suitable" for a community housing provider, and that Council's draft scheme "could equally preference monetary contributions".[^91] The Explanation adds that transfer to Homes NSW is being considered for Government-owned land in this or adjacent LGAs.[^84] **The safe statement is** that the documents say the contribution rate is "in perpetuity" but do not say how a dwelling held by Council, or bought with cash, is kept at an affordable rent, what "reasonable time" and "adjoining area" mean in practice, or what happens at the end of a provider's term. Those are the points I ask to see in the instrument.

**6.3** **What is at risk.** My estimate, from Census 2021, the Department of Communities and Justice rent tables and the Strategy's capacity figures, is that about 1,400 of the 2,862 removed homes are rented. About half the occupied dwellings in Edgecliff are rented, 70% are flats and 43% of those are studios or one-bedrooms;[^92] Council counts "close to 500" small strata units on the change sites, on floor area and not on rent;[^93] and Anglicare's 2026 snapshot found no affordable rental for an income-support household among 927 Eastern Suburbs listings.[^94] The definition of "affordable" uses the Sydney-wide median income while local rents are far above it, so most cheap local rentals fail the low-income test though they are cheap by local standards. A net-loss test built on the definition will therefore undercount the loss of cheap housing.

**6.4** **Which homes go first.** The Strategy says development occurs only where a site is "valuable enough to displace its existing uses", that "the less intensely improved ... the lower the price paid", and that strata titled blocks "can have favourable prospects", particularly those with relatively low site utilisation.[^95] Older, small, low-utilisation flats are the likeliest naturally occurring affordable housing, so the stock most likely to go first is disproportionately the low-cost stock. I matched eight key sites to street addresses through the Strategy's key-site test table and to Strata Hub registration dates; each includes a plan registered before 1990.[^96] Three are nil-rate in the Strategy's Table 4-1 and a fourth is shown at 0% on the Department's maps; two of the nil-rate sites, 62 Ocean Street and 2-4 New McLean Street, rank 9th and 14th of 156 on my screen. These links are my inferences, and the Department can confirm or reverse them by publishing the address of each key site. The nil rate compensates for public benefits (a link, a park, a station entry), which do not replace the housing lost.

**6.5** **The net effect.** Net change by 2046 is the affordable dwellings delivered in the precinct (in kind, plus cash-funded dwellings housed there by 2046) less the naturally occurring affordable housing lost. Appendices A2 to A4 set out the calculation and reproduce the table, which gives Monte Carlo medians with 5th to 95th percentile ranges across all parameters, including the exposure estimate:

| Build-out (net new homes) | Precinct, low-income test | Precinct, moderate-income test | Sydney-wide, low-income test | Sydney-wide, moderate-income test |
|---------------------------|--------------------------:|-------------------------------:|-----------------------------:|----------------------------------:|
| My central (about 1,350) | −13 (−64 to +12) | −77 (−192 to −17) | +15 (−34 to +61) | −35 (−141 to +35) |

**6.6** **How to read the table.** The sign is negative in 98% to 100% of draws at the moderate-income test in the precinct and in 70% to 86% at the low-income test, which is why I rely on the moderate-income test and treat the low-income result as unresolved. With cash the norm, about a tenth of the contribution is built on site and most of the cash is spent outside the precinct, while the loss of cheap homes is local. The households displaced are not the households housed. The "about 130 and 450" above are the deterministic central case; the Monte Carlo medians are higher (about 148 and 488). The main assumptions are the adverse-selection multiplier (older blocks build earlier; central 1.5, range 1.0 to 2.0), a 90% cash share (range 41% to 100%; the Department's consultant and the Economic Impact Assessment point toward cash), and 1.5 homes bought per dwelling-equivalent of cash (0.5 to 3.0), of which 15% are in the precinct (0% to 50%).

**6.7** **Where I have chosen assumptions that favour the Department.** I apply the Strategy's rate to net new homes (about 3.4 to 4.3 per 100), where its Table 5-1 applies it to gross capacity (432 on 12,262, 3.5 per 100 gross); using gross capacity would raise the contribution by about 30% and move the central moderate-income result from −84 to about −80, without changing the sign. I assume the most feasible sites build first, which raises the early contribution rate although two of the likeliest sites are nil-rate. And the table measures the proposal against no redevelopment, not against the Base Case, because no baseline is published.

**6.8** **Other schemes.** Schemes that price the uplift from rezoning ask for more: the City of Sydney's clause 7.13B takes 12% to 21% of new floor area.[^97] The safeguards the comparators carry and the exhibited scheme does not state are a stated base for the percentage; a term written into the instrument (as reported, Parramatta North, finalised in August 2026 on mostly government-owned land, requires 5% of homes on site, retained in perpetuity, in the clause itself[^98]); and replacement of demolished low-rent housing with a right to return (London Plan policy H8, Toronto Official Plan policy 3.2.1.6, Vancouver's one-for-one rule[^99]). These sit in different tenancy and ownership settings and are not like-for-like; I mention them to show the safeguards exist elsewhere, not that the Department must copy them.

**6.10** The cost side: one-for-one replacement adds about $67,000 per new home (about 2.5% of sale value, my arithmetic), which would use a tenth to a third of the headroom in the Department's own cases (section 5); a 12% rate would exceed it in most. Stricter rules reduce the number of sites that build, which also reduces both the loss and the supply gained. The Department has not tested that trade-off and is best placed to. The cheapest fixes (cash priced and ring-fenced, a stated rent rule and term, no nil rate where older flats are lost) cost little or nothing in headroom.

**6.11** **The Department's best counter-argument, which I accept in part.** New supply lowers rents a little across the city. Greater Sydney has about 1.77 million occupied dwellings, so 4,800 homes are about 0.27%; on supply elasticities from the literature (0.19 from German data, and about 0.34 implied by Grattan's modelling) that lowers average rents by about 0.05% to 0.09%, or $0.33 to $0.60 a week on a $650 rent, or about $11m to $20m a year across all renters (my arithmetic; Appendix A5).[^100] The aggregate is not trivial, but it is spread across about 627,000 rented households and is not targeted at those displaced. Filtering is real but small and slow: AHURI's study of Sydney and Melbourne supports supply but finds that "the evidence does not support relying on filtering as a substitute for the non-market provision of affordable housing".[^101] My estimate from a Sydney panel of 343 areas is about 3 (range 1 to 5) additional rented dwellings per 100 added dwellings below the real first-quartile rent, over five to ten years, in lower-income suburbs and not in Woollahra; in Double Bay - Darling Point, whose towers are 45 to 65 years old, only 6% of rentals are below Sydney's first-quartile rent (25% across Sydney).[^92] I found no evidence for a longer-run gain. This is why I concede that supply helps and argue for a stated rent rule and term, replacement and staging, not against supply.

**6.12** **Harms of a precinct with little affordable housing.** Of the 21,058 people who work in Woollahra LGA, 65.8% live outside it, and 75% of those in local-service industries,[^102] and Woollahra has the lowest concentration of essential-worker residents in Greater Sydney, 0.37 of the labour-force average.[^103] The Department's own documents say the new homes are "likely to be out of reach of average income earners".[^104] I have not tried to price this. In my experience, running a local school-age care service, senior staff cannot afford to live near the school, which costs them and adds to traffic; it is one organisation and an illustration only. Proximity to Edgecliff, Double Bay and Bondi Junction shortens commutes, and volunteering rates in Woollahra are above the Sydney average (19.9% against 12.4%),[^92] so I do not overstate it.

**6.13** **Anticipating the Department's response.** First, that the Strategy found 3% (up to 6% within 200 m of Woollahra Station) tolerable on the sites it tested.[^105] Tolerable to developers is not the same as sufficient for the housing lost. Secondly, that the Low and Mid-Rise provisions also remove cheap housing, so the Base Case already has the loss; the relevant question is the increment, which I cannot compute without the baseline. Thirdly, that nil rates reflect public benefits delivered; a replacement duty is a different thing. Fourthly, that Council ownership answers the duration question; it answers part of it, and I ask how such a dwelling is kept at an affordable rent.

**6.14** **What I ask.** Requests R6.1 to R6.7 in the schedule.

## 7. One site I know: 400 Glenmore Road

**7.1** **Why this site, and how I put it.** This is an area I know very well, and I use this site as an example because the proposal's general gaps meet on it. It has several independent problems; together they call for assessments the exhibition does not contain. My point is not that height here is unthinkable, but that the interface is unassessed and the departure is large.

**7.2** **The identification is an inference.** No Government document gives the lot's address. I identify key site TR.1.1 with 400 Glenmore Road because it is the only north-west tip lot proposed as R4 on the maps, because Council names 400 Glenmore Road (strata plan SP39751) as an R3 to R4 rezoning and describes no other lot there, and because the Department's own list of lots contains SP39751.[^106] The Department can settle it in one line and I ask it to.

### The site and the scale of the change

**7.3** **The Department's documents.** TR.1.1 is proposed at 74.2 m and a floor space ratio of 2.2:1, shown as a 22-storey tower (about 22 at 3.34 m a storey, my arithmetic) on a four-storey street wall with a 10 m setback on its Glenmore Road edge, with open space the only ticked requirement.[^107] The lot is the strata scheme SP39751, with unit numbers up to 52/400 (48 unit rows in the Department's lot list, so at least 52 units)[^108] and an area of about 6,238 m² (NSW Spatial Services cadastre, accessed 24 October 2026). The current controls on the lot are R3, a height limit of 13.5 m and a floor space ratio of 0.75:1.[^109] My arithmetic: the tower is about 5.5 times the height limit and the floor space ratio is about 2.9 times the current one. I have not been able to establish whether the Low and Mid-Rise provisions (which carry a 2.2:1 floor space ratio in some locations) apply to this lot; if they do, the floor space comparison is to 2.2:1 and the height comparison is unaffected.

**7.4** **What is there now.** In my experience the present building is two storeys. If so, the tower is about ten times the height of what is there now (two storeys of about 7 m, my arithmetic), and a floor space ratio of 2.2:1 allows about 9,000 m² more floor area than 0.75:1, roughly 70 to 90 more homes at 100 to 130 m² each (my estimate; the Department has not published its count). I give both comparisons, the limit and the building, because the Department may reasonably compare the tower with either. The building directly opposite is five levels, two of parking and three of apartments, a former car repair premises converted to preserve the building, with about 120 unit addresses. It is not heritage listed. So the street is not uniformly low. But five storeys is about 15 to 17 m, a fifth to a quarter of the proposed height, and that building was retained and adapted, not replaced.

**7.5** **The mitigating factors, and why the departure remains large.** The Department can point to a tower that is the lowest of the three at Trumper Park (74.2 m against 78.1 m and 90.2 m), a four-storey street wall, a drawn setback, a new 3,238 m² park, and the Heritage Report's description of the northern and eastern fringes of the conservation area as "less visually intact".[^110] I accept those facts. After all of them the departure is still about 5.5 times the limit and, on my observation, about ten times the building there. The Urban Design Report describes "the most significant setback departure" as a 10 m setback established along Glenmore Road.[^111] The Design Guide's Figure 33 shows 10 m on the Glenmore Road edge.[^112] I have not been able to confirm whether the setback is the same on every edge facing the street, and I ask the Department to state it for each edge.

### Heritage, character and precedent

**7.6** **The lot is inside the conservation area, not on its edge.** The Urban Design Report says the Trumper Park area "sits entirely within the Paddington Heritage Conservation Area" and the Design Guide says it is "also within" it.[^113] The Planning Portal's heritage layer places the whole lot, and the building opposite, inside it, with the nearest boundary about 99 m away.[^114] The same report excludes areas within the Paddington conservation area from change because small lots in fragmented ownership "preclude site amalgamations".[^115] The exhibition treats this lot inconsistently with that reasoning.

**7.7** **No heritage assessment of the lot.** The Heritage Report gives Trumper Park one focus area, at the Quarry Street edge (item I499, which the Urban Design Report identifies as 238 Jersey Road), and I found no assessment, view or advice that names 400 Glenmore Road.[^116] It says site-specific interface controls should be resolved at rezoning stage and that heritage issues cannot be left to later development applications alone.[^117] The Design Guide's text asks only for "larger setbacks, for example along Glenmore Road" and gives no number; its Figure 33 shows a 10 m setback on that edge.[^118] The Visual Impact Assessment has fourteen viewpoints, none on a street inside the Paddington conservation area south of Trumper Park (VP14 is on Jersey Road, at the area's edge), and sets aside heritage and private-domain views.[^119]

**7.8** **The precedent risk.** I put this as a risk and a question, not a certainty. If a tower of this scale is made permissible inside the conservation area, the reasoning offered (that this is the "less visually intact" fringe, with taller buildings already along Jersey Road, Glenmore Road and south of Trumper Park) would apply to every neighbouring and similar lot, so that each intrusion justifies the next. Once the controls permit a tower, the "desired future character" for neighbouring lots shifts, and later applications can point to it. Other conservation areas with high-rise edges, in Woollahra and elsewhere in Sydney, would be open to the same argument. And because the Heritage Report says the interface should be settled at rezoning stage, there is no later point at which the rule can be set.

**7.9** **Privacy.** I do not raise privacy as an objection. The separation between the tower face and the building opposite is about 20.5 to 24.5 m across the road, depending on the setback (my estimate), and the tower would be further from it than the existing building is.

### Flood

**7.10** **Council's advice, as Council's.** Council's flood referral response, which is based on a consultant memorandum, advises that rezoning 400 Glenmore Road from R3 to R4 "is not supported due to its location within a floodway and high flood hazard ('H5' category) for the 5% AEP event and above", and recommend: "Exclude 400 Glenmore Road, Paddington ... from the rezoning proposal due to its location on a floodway and being subject to high hazard flood risk."[^120] That is Council's advice, not an independent finding.

**7.11** **The Government's own strategy.** The Flooding Report's strategy is to "avoid development in the floodway". It says of the areas around Trumper Park that "no rezoning or development is proposed here", and it proposes no rezoning between Trumper Park and Hampden Street because depths exceed 1 m.[^121] Yet Trumper Park carries three R4 sites at 74.2 m to 90.2 m.[^122] The report names Glenmore Road once, as an overland flow path, and never names this lot.[^123] The Urban Design Report's flood-constraint map shows the tip as "High Constraint" (my reading of the image), and its text lists "reducing or removing basement parking" among general responses for flood-affected sites.[^124] The Flooding Report does not say the three R4 sites are within the areas "around Trumper Park" where it proposes no development; it does not say they are outside them either. That is a question for the Department to answer, not an admission.

**7.12** **What the Department's maps show at the lot (my reading of the images; the lot is not drawn on them, so this is approximate).** High hazard (H5) covers a minor part of the lot, mostly along the road edge, in the 5% and 1% events, and most of the lot in the probable maximum flood, where a substantial part is also two metres or deeper.[^125] Council's wording is stronger than the Department's maps in the 5% event. I do not know why the two accounts differ, and the Department has not reconciled them. I do not say that the whole lot is high hazard.

**7.13** **What follows.** The primary flood strategy is shelter in place, with flash flooding in under 30 minutes and a statement that "adequate warning times would not be available".[^126] The post-development results assume every stormwater upgrade is built, and the Infrastructure Delivery Plan puts all of them ten or more years out (section 4). The Woollahra Development Control Plan treats high-hazard flood areas and floodways as unsuitable for development unless a flood risk report is prepared, and requires fully tanked basements.[^127] The Geotechnical Assessment says the low-lying area where Trumper Oval now stands was originally swamp, then a rubbish dump, then filled (p 25), and that basement excavation in low-lying areas commonly intersects groundwater (p 8); it does not say whether 400 Glenmore Road is in that area.[^128] The Design Guide's parking rates are maximums, so a basement is not required (about 117 spaces would be allowed for about 130 homes at 0.9 a home, my arithmetic),[^129] but no flood-safe parking and access solution is shown for a 22-storey tower, nor what removing the basement does to the yield. I ask for a flood-resilience assessment, not for a conclusion that it cannot be done.

### Sun

**7.14** **No analysis of existing homes.** The solar test in the Urban Design Report measures sunlight on open space, not on any existing dwelling. The Urban Design Report and Design Guide both state the principle "Protect solar access to existing dwellings", and neither gives a test for it; the Urban Design Report says analysis of the effect on existing adjacent dwellings is still to come.[^130]

**7.15** **My estimate, as an upper-end sensitivity (Appendix A6).** The building opposite lies to the south of the lot, so a tower shades it in every position I tested. No tower form is exhibited, so I assumed a four-storey podium over most of the lot with a tower on top, and I tried floor plates of 450, 600 and 750 m² in three positions. The model is generous: the podium and tower together exceed the floor area the exhibited ratio allows on the lot, so the true effect is more likely to sit at the lower end. On 21 June the tower newly shades the building from 9:00 until between about 12:30 and 14:50 depending on its size and position. Winter sun on its north façade falls by about 11% to 21% (mean sun-hours, on a terrain model, relative to an assumed existing 12 m building; less on flat ground): about 11% to 15% at 450 m², 13% to 18% at 600 m², and 14% to 21% at 750 m². Only the largest plate in the southernmost position reaches the 20% figure in the Apartment Design Guide's design guidance for objective 3B-2. I understand that figure applies where the neighbour does not already receive the required hours, and I do not know which case applies here, so I do not claim a breach. My point is that the effect is of a size that needs checking, the Department holds the real tower form, and no analysis of existing homes is exhibited. I ask the Department to publish its own shadow diagrams.

### The junction and the street

**7.16** **The case is not that the tower alone swamps Glenmore Road.** The tower adds about 2 to 30 trips in a peak hour, central about 15 (my estimate, with assumed trip rates and home counts; Appendix A6). The case is the combination at the New South Head Road end of the street.

**7.17** **Four layers, and what is verified.** (1) *Geometry.* In my experience, near New South Head Road cars parked on both sides narrow the road. The queuing I generally experience is at weekends, and it is irregular, which is the point: a blockage near the exit that is not cleared quickly tends to back up, and it is slow to clear because New South Head Road is heavy. I have no dated record of it. (2) *The club.* A sports and social club was approved on appeal in 2021 beside the site.[^131] I understand it has a driveway exit onto Glenmore Road; the approved plans would confirm it. The Government's documents do not mention the club's development (the transport plan names White City Tennis only as a recreation area, p 24), and the transport plan's base is not shown to include it. (3) *Growth.* The rezoning adds about 2,100 homes at Edgecliff,[^46] and growth is proposed or under way at Rose Bay.[^132] (4) *The tower.* Only then. No document assesses these together.

**7.18** **What the documents do not do.** The only junction study the transport plan cites for New South Head Road, Glenmore Road and Mona Road is from 2023 and was run for 500 to 600 dwellings, about 5% to 6% of the 9,400 proposed (my arithmetic).[^133] It found the junction "satisfactory" in all peak hours, with the Mona Road approach at level of service F in the afternoon peak. I accept that finding for the load it tested.[^133] The plan also shows the junction's existing 2023 performance for the morning, afternoon and weekend peaks, and flags the Mona Road approach as an exception in all three.[^134] I have found no queue-length survey, no observed count at the Glenmore Road exit, and no test at the combined load described above, and the plan's list of five congestion locations does not include this junction.[^135] The plan itself says no current studies have assessed intersections in the Woollahra area (p 53). A modelled junction result would not show queues, recovery from a blockage, or the parked-car effects I describe.

**7.19** **The network role, and what I do not claim.** On a map of public streets I find only two leaving the south side of New South Head Road between Neild Avenue and Ocean Avenue: Glenmore Road and New McLean Street. The transport plan proposes closing New McLean Street to vehicles and lists a new New McLean Street to Glenmore Road connection alongside, saying the local road changes are "unlikely to change the role and function of key road corridors".[^136] I do not claim that traffic would route through Glenmore Road as a result. My point that Glenmore Road is the only significant exit in that stretch is largely supported. But in a static route test to a city destination, only about 1.5% to 2.8% of Paddington's dwellings (97 to 183 of 6,435) have this connection on their fastest or shortest route, and none do on the shortest route to the airport (my estimate, not observed traffic). So I do not say that large parts of Paddington depend on it, and I do not say the junction fails. The case rests on the choke point and the combined load. I ask for a study of how the junction actually behaves, whatever happens to this site: observed surveys (counts, queue lengths, and how often and for how long the road becomes blocked), at weekday, evening, weekend and event times and long enough to catch irregular weekend blockages, followed by modelling of the combined load.

### Ownership, and what I ask for the site

**7.20** **Assembly.** Replacing a strata scheme of at least 52 units needs the owners' agreement. No document says how many owners there are, what they have been told, or what happens if they decline, and the Department's own admissions on assembly apply (section 5).

**7.21** **Anticipating the Department's response.**

(b) Failing that, retain the existing controls on the lot or allow at most a modest increase (for example four or five storeys, in line with the building opposite) that does not require an R4 rezoning, if the assessments show that resolves the problems. A scale-back largely answers heritage scale, precedent and overshadowing. (c) Failing that, no approval of height until the following are published and exhibited: a lot overlay on the flood maps and an answer to Council by address; a flood-resilience assessment with an evacuation test; shadow diagrams for existing homes; a heritage and visual assessment from named Paddington streets with numeric interface controls; a junction and street assessment based on observed surveys and including the club; the dwellings removed and added, and which key site this is (R7.1 to R7.8).

**7.23** **What I would not say.** That the lot is high hazard across its whole area; that privacy is breached; that traffic from Edgecliff would route through Glenmore Road; that the junction fails; that large parts of Paddington rely on this junction; that the tower alone would swamp Glenmore Road; or that the building opposite is heritage listed.

```python
Calibration: the rebuild of the Strategy's method needs a single factor of 1.15 on sale values (price=1.15 below) to fit the plotted points.
The screen uses one generic land-cost band and interpolates the $4,500-6,500 cost range linearly from 4 to 30 storeys (see section 5).
Usage: python3 a1_keysite_screen.py "Edgecliff-Woollahra+Explanation+of+Intended+Effect.pdf"
Needs poppler's pdftotext. Python 3.8+, standard library only."""
import re, subprocess, sys
from collections import Counter

COLS = [(53, 'link'), (77, 'vehicle'), (99, 'road'), (116, 'drain'), (135, 'open'), (150, 'community'), (173, 'afford')]  # tick x-positions

# 1. Key-site table: EIE Appendix D, PDF pp 39-43 (block ID, FSR, height in m).
sites = []
for pg in range(39, 44):
    txt = subprocess.run(['pdftotext', '-layout', '-f', str(pg), '-l', str(pg), sys.argv[1], '-'],
                         capture_output=True, text=True, check=True).stdout
    for line in txt.split('\n'):
        line = line.replace('C S .', 'CS.')
        m = re.match(r'\s*([A-Z ]{2,4}?)\.(\d+)\.(\d+)\s+(.*)', line)
        if m:
            nums = [float(x.replace(' ', '')) for x in re.findall(r'\d+\s*\.\s*\d+|\d+', re.sub(r'◆|\d+%', '', m.group(4)))]
            h = [n for n in nums[1:] if n > 14]
            req = {min(COLS, key=lambda c: abs(c[0] - x.start()))[1] for x in re.finditer('◆', line)}   # requirement ticks
            sites.append(dict(id=m.group(1).replace(' ', '') + f'.{m.group(2)}.{m.group(3)}', area=m.group(1).replace(' ', ''),

# 2. Residual land value (RLV) per m2 of site area, rebuilt from the Affordable Housing Strategy Schedule 2 (PDF pp 57-60).
def rlv(fsr, storeys, near200=False, tpc=False, price=1.15, cost=1.0, margin=0.20, pre=12, build=30, nsa=0.85, car=120000):
    mix = dict(b1=.30, b2=.50, b3=.20) if near200 else dict(b1=.10, b2=.40, b3=.50)        # unit mix (AHS p59)
    size, px, park = dict(b1=65, b2=80, b3=120), dict(b1=23000, b2=26000, b3=29000), dict(b1=.6, b2=.9, b3=1.4)
    avg = sum(mix[k] * size[k] for k in mix)
    p_nsa = sum(mix[k] * size[k] * px[k] for k in mix) / avg                                  # $/m2 NSA, mid-points (AHS p60)
    n_sa = fsr * nsa
    dwell = n_sa / avg
    rev = n_sa * p_nsa * price / 1.1                                                          # GST included in end values (AHS p60)
    sell = rev * 0.025
    rate = (4500 + min(max((storeys - 4) / 26, 0), 1) * 2000) * cost                         # $4,500-6,500 per m2 building area
    hard = fsr * 1.15 * rate + 0.10 * n_sa * 1000 + dwell * sum(mix[k] * park[k] for k in mix) * car
    soft = hard * (0.10 + 0.05 + 0.0025)
    dev = hard + soft + (hard + soft) * 0.04 + dwell * (10812 + (15000 if tpc else 0))      # fees, contingency, 4% levy, HPC/TPC
    C = dev + dev * 0.06 * (build / 12) * 0.55 + dev * 0.0035 + sell                         # finance: 55% average drawn, 6%
    load = 1.055 * (1 + 0.06 * (pre + build) / 12)                                            # land transaction cost and interest
    return (rev / (1 + margin) - C) / load

for f in (1.0, 0.5, 0.2):

for feas in (1.0, 0.6):                                                                   # State's "4% of feasible capacity a year", 20 years
```

```python

net = affordable dwellings delivered in the precinct (in kind, plus cash-funded dwellings housed there by 2046)
      less naturally occurring affordable housing (NOAH) lost when its building is redeveloped.
LOW test:      lam * gain - NOAH_low_lost       MODERATE test: gain - NOAH_mod_lost
Inputs not estimated in this file: NOAH exposure at full build-out (130 LOW, 450 MODERATE, 1,397 rented dwellings among the 2,862

    ALPHA=(3.0, 3.5, 5.5),    # affordable dwelling-equivalents per 100 net new homes (3% of what is not stated: 3.0 to 5.5)
```

**A3. Exposure: low-rent homes among the removed dwellings (section 6)**

```python
"""A3. Naturally occurring affordable housing (NOAH) among the removed dwellings (indicative; own arithmetic).
Python standard library plus numpy. Run from the folder holding data/ (scripts A2-A4 are run from one folder): python3 a3_noah_exposure.py
Removed (exposed) dwellings = gross capacity less net new homes, by character area (exhibited figures); x flat share x rented share
x share of rents at or below the weekly rent threshold (30% of the LOW / MODERATE income limit), from Census 2021 proxy suburbs.
Not a forecast of demolitions: lost NOAH = exposure x build-out share (A2/A4). Ranges are judgemental, not confidence intervals.
Data files (data/):
  capacity_by_area.csv   columns area, ahs_gross, udr_uplift, udr_existing (one row per character area; a TOTAL row is skipped).
                         NSW DPHI Affordable Housing Strategy Table 5-1 (gross capacity) and Urban Design Report pp86-87 (net new, existing)
  census_tenure.csv      ABS Census 2021 (ABS Data API; tenure by dwelling structure, suburb SAL): name, year, occupied, rented, rented_flats, flats_occupied
  census_flat_beds.csv   ABS Census 2021 (Data API, dwelling structure x bedrooms): name, flats, flats_0bed, flats_1bed, flats_2bed, flats_3plus, flats_bed_not_stated
  census_rent_bands.csv  ABS Census 2021 (Data API, weekly rent bands, rented dwellings): name, year, band, lo, hi, n (hi of top band is open: 1e9)"""
import csv, numpy as np

def read(f): return list(csv.DictReader(open('data/' + f)))
# --- parameters: name = (low, central, high) or value; source / assumption on each line
COUNCIL_REMOVED = 2860     # Woollahra Council submission p130: dwellings on the 653 change-site properties (method not given)
INCOME = dict(LOW=(60400, 30200, 18100), MODERATE=(90700, 45400, 27200))   # NSW income limits 2026/27, Sydney: single, each extra adult, each child ($/yr)
RENT_SHARE = 0.30          # affordable rent = 30% of gross household income (NSW Affordable Housing Guidelines)
PROXY = {'Edgecliff Centre': ['Edgecliff (SAL)'], 'Trumper Park': ['Paddington NSW (SAL)'], 'Plateau': ['Woollahra (SAL)'],
         'Woollahra Centre': ['Woollahra (SAL)'], 'Cliff Edge': ['Edgecliff (SAL)', 'Woollahra (SAL)'],
         'Queen Street': ['Woollahra (SAL)'], 'East Woollahra': ['Woollahra (SAL)']}   # nearest-suburb Census proxy for each area; JUDGEMENT
CENTRAL = dict(
    METHOD='A',       # removed dwellings: A gross less net; B Council 2,860 pro rata to existing; C Council 2,860 pro rata to gross capacity
    TAKE_UP=0.51,     # unused here (A2/A4 apply build-out share s*M instead); EIA pp33-34 proposal case 4,800/9,400
    REMOVED_MULT=1.0, # structural uncertainty on total removed; JUDGEMENT (exhibited figures share one origin)
    F_FLAT=0.85,      # share of removed dwellings that are flats (2,860 on 653 properties = 4.4 each); JUDGEMENT
    RENTAL_TILT=1.0,  # multiplier on suburb rented share (older investor blocks vs average); JUDGEMENT
    RENT_GROWTH=1.26, # in-place rent growth Census 2021 to mid-2026: ABS CPI Rents 81.32 to 102.29 (own arithmetic)
    PAIRING='strict', # strict: 1-bed = single adult, 2-bed = couple; loose: couple / couple plus child
    HOUSE_LOW=0.0,    # share of rented houses and semis that is NOAH, LOW test; JUDGEMENT
    HOUSE_MOD=0.03)   # same, MODERATE test; JUDGEMENT
RANGES = dict(REMOVED_MULT=(.8, 1.2), TAKE_UP=(.14, 1.0), F_FLAT=(.70, .95), RENTAL_TILT=(.85, 1.15), RENT_GROWTH=(1.10, 1.60),
              HOUSE_LOW=(0., .03), HOUSE_MOD=(0., .08))   # uniform draws; JUDGEMENT. TAKE_UP is drawn but unused here (keeps the random stream aligned with A4)

def rent_threshold(band, pairing):
    """Weekly rent thresholds (1-bed, 2-bed): 30% of income limit / 52."""
    s, a, c = INCOME[band]
    people = dict(strict=(s, s + a), loose=(s + a, s + a + c))[pairing]
    return [RENT_SHARE * x / 52 for x in people]

TEN = {r['name']: r for r in read('census_tenure.csv') if r['year'] == '2021'}
BEDS = {r['name']: r for r in read('census_flat_beds.csv')}
BANDS = {}
for r in read('census_rent_bands.csv'):
    if r['year'] == '2021': BANDS.setdefault(r['name'], []).append((float(r['lo']), float(r['hi']), float(r['n'])))
CAP = {r['area']: r for r in read('capacity_by_area.csv') if r['area'] != 'TOTAL'}

def removed(method):
    g = {a: float(r['ahs_gross']) for a, r in CAP.items()}; e = {a: float(r['udr_existing']) for a, r in CAP.items()}
    if method == 'A': return {a: g[a] - float(CAP[a]['udr_uplift']) for a in CAP}
    k = e if method == 'B' else g
    return {a: COUNCIL_REMOVED * k[a] / sum(k.values()) for a in CAP}

def cdf(bands, t):
    """Share of stated-rent rented dwellings paying <= t a week (linear within a band)."""
    tot = sum(n for _, _, n in bands)
    return sum(n if z <= t else n * (t - a) / (z - a) if a < t else 0. for a, z, n in bands) / tot

def estimate(P=CENTRAL):
    """Return exposed, rented, NOAH-LOW and NOAH-MODERATE dwellings, summed over areas."""
    rem, th = removed(P['METHOD']), {b: rent_threshold(b, P['PAIRING']) for b in INCOME}
    tot = dict(exposed=0., rented=0., low=0., mod=0.)
    for a in CAP:
        flats = rem[a] * P['REMOVED_MULT'] * P['F_FLAT']; houses = rem[a] * P['REMOVED_MULT'] - flats
        rf = rh = 0.; share = dict(LOW=0., MODERATE=0.); n = len(PROXY[a])
        for nm in PROXY[a]:
            t, b = TEN[nm], BEDS[nm]; fl = float(b['flats']) - float(b['flats_bed_not_stated'])
            w01 = (float(b['flats_0bed']) + float(b['flats_1bed'])) / fl
            rf += min(1., float(t['rented_flats']) / float(t['flats_occupied']) * P['RENTAL_TILT']) / n
            rh += min(1., (float(t['rented']) - float(t['rented_flats'])) / (float(t['occupied']) - float(t['flats_occupied'])) * P['RENTAL_TILT']) / n
            for k in share:   # thresholds are deflated to Census-2021 rents
                share[k] += (w01 * cdf(BANDS[nm], th[k][0] / P['RENT_GROWTH']) + (1 - w01) * cdf(BANDS[nm], th[k][1] / P['RENT_GROWTH'])) / n
        tot['exposed'] += flats + houses; tot['rented'] += flats * rf + houses * rh
        tot['low'] += flats * rf * share['LOW'] + houses * rh * P['HOUSE_LOW']
        tot['mod'] += flats * rf * share['MODERATE'] + houses * rh * P['HOUSE_MOD']
    return tot

def draw_exposure(n=5000, seed=1):
    """Monte Carlo over RANGES plus discrete method and pairing; returns an array of (exposed, rented, low, mod)."""
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed); out = []
    for _ in range(n):
        P = dict(CENTRAL, **{k: float(rng.uniform(*v)) for k, v in RANGES.items()})
        P['METHOD'] = str(rng.choice(['A', 'B', 'C'])); P['PAIRING'] = str(rng.choice(['strict', 'loose']))
        e = estimate(P); out.append((e['exposed'], e['rented'], e['low'], e['mod']))
    return np.array(out)

if __name__ == '__main__':
    print('thresholds $/wk 2026/27 strict (1-bed, 2-bed):', {b: [round(x) for x in rent_threshold(b, 'strict')] for b in INCOME})
    c = estimate(); print(f"removed = {sum(float(r['ahs_gross']) for r in CAP.values()):,.0f} - {sum(float(r['udr_uplift']) for r in CAP.values()):,.0f} = {c['exposed']:,.0f}")
    d = draw_exposure(); q = lambda i: '%.0f (%.0f to %.0f)' % tuple(np.percentile(d[:, i], [50, 5, 95]))
    for lab, i, k in (('rented among removed', 1, 'rented'), ('NOAH LOW test', 2, 'low'), ('NOAH MODERATE test', 3, 'mod')):
        print(f'{lab:22s} central {c[k]:6.0f} | Monte Carlo median (5th to 95th) {q(i)}')
```

```python
import random, statistics as st
import a3_noah_exposure as A3

N = 9400                   # net new homes on change sites (Urban Design Report pp86-87)
# name: (low, central, high); order matters (it fixes the random stream)
```

**A5a. Sydney panel (section 6)**

```python
"""A5a. Sydney SA2 panel: rented dwellings below the real 2021 first-quartile rent vs added dwellings.
Needs: Python 3 standard library + numpy only (OLS and SA3-clustered errors are coded below).
Run:   python3 a5a_panel.py [DATA_DIR]      (DATA_DIR default ./data)
Inputs (SDMX-CSV from the ABS Data API, https://data.api.abs.gov.au; ABS Census 2011/2016/2021 time-series
  tables on 2021 SA2 boundaries; flow IDs as named; 'gsyd' = Greater Sydney GCCSA 1GSYD):
  comp_c21_t02_{sa2,gsyd}.csv  C21_T02_SA2  medians (MEDAVG 4 = household income $/wk, 6 = rent $/wk)
  comp_c21_t18_sa2.csv         C21_T18_SA2  occupied private dwellings by tenure (TENLLD) and structure (STRD)
  comp_c21_t19_{sa2,gsyd}.csv  C21_T19_SA2  rented dwellings by weekly rent band (RNTRD 1-14; 14 = $950+)
  comp_region_names.json       ABS ASGS 2021 codelist (code -> name), used only to find Double Bay - Darling Point.
"""
import csv, json, os, sys
import numpy as np

RAW = sys.argv[1] if len(sys.argv) > 1 else 'data'
YEARS = (2011, 2016, 2021)
# ABS T19 band lower edges, $/week (band 1 is "nil rent", 14 is "$950 and over")  [ABS Census table definition]
LO = [1, 75, 100, 150, 200, 225, 275, 350, 450, 550, 650, 750, 850, 950]
HI = LO[1:] + [1e9]
TOP_CAP = 1200          # open-ended top band is cut at $1,200 when finding a quantile  [repo assumption]
MIN_OCC = 500           # drop SA2s with fewer than 500 occupied dwellings at start of period  [repo sample rule]
HIGH_COST_Q = 2 / 3     # high-cost = top tercile of SA2 median rent at start of period, within period  [repo rule]
# Own classification of SA3s (first 5 digits of SA2 code) into inner / middle ring; all others "outer"  [repo list]
INNER = {'11701', '11702', '11703', '11801', '11802', '12001', '12002', '12104'}
MIDDLE = {'11901', '11902', '11903', '11904', '12003', '12101', '12102', '12103', '12201', '12202', '12203',
          '12501', '12502', '12503', '12504', '12601', '12602', '12801', '12802'}


def rows(name):
    with open(os.path.join(RAW, name)) as f:
        for r in csv.DictReader(f):
            try:
                r['v'] = float(r['OBS_VALUE']); r['y'] = int(r['TIME_PERIOD'])
            except ValueError:
                continue
            yield r


def below(counts, t):
    """Rented dwellings under rent t: bands wholly below count fully; the band containing t is cut linearly
    (assumes rents are uniform within a band)."""
    return sum(n * min(1.0, max(0.0, (t - LO[k]) / (HI[k] - LO[k]))) for k, n in counts.items())


def quantile(counts, q):
    tot, cum = sum(counts.values()), 0.0
    for k in sorted(counts):
        if cum + counts[k] >= q * tot:
            return LO[k] + (q * tot - cum) / counts[k] * (min(HI[k], TOP_CAP) - LO[k])
        cum += counts[k]


def bands(sa2=None):
    """{(region, year): {band_index: n}} from T19 (all landlord types, LLDD = _T)."""
    out = {}
    for r in rows('comp_c21_t19_%s.csv' % ('sa2' if sa2 else 'gsyd')):
        if r['RNTRD'] in {str(i) for i in range(1, 15)}:
            out.setdefault((r['REGION'], r['y']), {})[int(r['RNTRD']) - 1] = r['v']
    return out


def thresholds():
    """Greater Sydney 2021 first-quartile rent, deflated to 2011/2016 by the ratio of Greater Sydney median rents."""
    g = bands(); med = {r['y']: r['v'] for r in rows('comp_c21_t02_gsyd.csv') if r['MEDAVG'] == '6'}
    q1 = quantile(g[('1GSYD', 2021)], 0.25)
    return {y: q1 * med[y] / med[2021] for y in YEARS}, q1


def panel():
    thr, _ = thresholds(); b = bands(sa2=True)

def ols_cluster(y, X, cl):
    """OLS with CR1 cluster-robust variance (G/(G-1) * (n-1)/(n-k))."""
    n, k = X.shape; XtXi = np.linalg.pinv(X.T @ X); b = XtXi @ X.T @ y; e = y - X @ b
    meat = sum(np.outer(s, s) for s in (X[cl == g].T @ e[cl == g] for g in set(cl)))
    G = len(set(cl)); V = XtXi @ meat @ XtXi * (G / (G - 1)) * ((n - 1) / (n - k))
    return b, np.sqrt(np.diag(V)), G
```

**A5b. Vacancy chain and city-wide rent effect (section 6)**

```python
"""A5b. Vacancy-chain model (affordable dwellings freed per 100 new market dwellings) and city-wide rent effect.
Needs: Python 3 standard library only, plus a5a_panel.py (same folder) for Greater Sydney dwelling counts.
Run:   python3 a5b_chain.py [DATA_DIR]
Inputs: ABS Census 2021 T18 (occupied and rented dwellings, Greater Sydney SA2s) via a5a_panel.py; every other
number is a named parameter below, with its source. Method: per 100 new dwellings, vacancies in round r are
100*V*G**r (r = 0..5); the bottom-quintile share B[r] of them are in bottom-quintile areas; each becomes a rental
with probability T, is re-let under the threshold with probability A, and survives un-renovated with 1-U.
"""
import random, statistics as st
import a5a_panel as a

B = [0.10, 0.15, 0.20, 0.25, 0.30, 0.30]   # bottom-quintile share of movers by round: Bratu, Harjunen & Saarimaa 2023 (10% rising to 30%)
# (low, central, high); Monte Carlo draws are triangular(low, high, mode=central), 20,000 draws, seed 1
V = (0.5, 0.75, 0.9)        # share of new-unit occupants who vacate a dwelling in region: Bratu et al.; low is own sensitivity
G = (0.5, 0.63, 0.75)       # chain continuation per round: about 75% of chains end within 3 rounds (Hauck & Kluser)
T = (0.35, 0.45, 0.6)       # share of vacated dwellings re-let as rentals: ASSUMPTION (Sydney renters about 36%, flats higher)
A_LOW = (0.0, 0.03, 0.10)   # share of re-lets under LOW rent: NSW DCJ Rent and Sales Report, Jun-2026 new bonds, Greater Sydney flats
A_MOD = (0.11, 0.25, 0.41)  # share under MODERATE rent: same DCJ source (1-bed single 11%; 2-bed couple 41%)
U = (0.10, 0.20, 0.40)      # share renovated/removed before filtering: ASSUMPTION, AHURI Final Report 387 development-application proxy; judgement, range 10% to 40%
DRAWS, SEED = 20000, 1
ELASTICITY = {'Mense 2025': 0.19, 'Grattan (approx.)': 0.34}  # % fall in average rent per 1% rise in supply; Mense (2025, Germany); Grattan 2025 (12% for ~67,000 homes a year over 10 years)
RENT_WEEK, NEW_HOMES = 650, 4800   # typical weekly rent, ASSUMPTION; precinct net new homes, Department's Explanation of Intended Effect


def affordable(p):
    freed = 100 * p['V'] * sum(p['G'] ** r * B[r] for r in range(6))
    base = freed * p['T'] * (1 - p['U'])
    return freed, base * p['A_LOW'], base * p['A_MOD']


def pct(v, x): return sorted(v)[int(x * (len(v) - 1))]


if __name__ == '__main__':
    P = dict(V=V, G=G, T=T, A_LOW=A_LOW, A_MOD=A_MOD, U=U)
    f, lo, mo = affordable({k: v[1] for k, v in P.items()})
    print('central: vacancies in bottom-quintile areas %.1f per 100; freed LOW %.2f MODERATE %.2f' % (f, lo, mo))
    rng = random.Random(SEED); L, M = [], []
    for _ in range(DRAWS):
        _, x, y = affordable({k: rng.triangular(v[0], v[2], v[1]) for k, v in P.items()}); L.append(x); M.append(y)
    for n, v in (('LOW', L), ('MODERATE', M)):
        print('Monte Carlo %s: median %.2f (5th-95th %.2f to %.2f)' % (n, st.median(v), pct(v, .05), pct(v, .95)))
    cum, w = 0, [G[1] ** r * B[r] for r in range(6)]
    print('cumulative share by round 1-5: ' + ', '.join('%.0f%%' % (100 * sum(w[:i]) / sum(w)) for i in range(1, 6)))
    p = a.panel(); occ = sum(d['occ'] for (s, y), d in p.items() if y == 2021); rented = sum(d['rented'] for (s, y), d in p.items() if y == 2021)
    sh = NEW_HOMES / occ
    print('%d homes = %.2f%% of %s occupied dwellings (%s rented)' % (NEW_HOMES, 100 * sh, format(int(occ), ','), format(int(rented), ',')))
    for n, e in ELASTICITY.items():
        print('  %s: rent -%.3f%% = $%.2f a week on $%d; $%.1fm a year across rented dwellings' % (n, 100 * sh * e, RENT_WEEK * sh * e, RENT_WEEK, rented * RENT_WEEK * 52 * sh * e / 1e6))
```

```python
tower-to-357 separation. Python 3 + numpy only (no shapely).
Run:  python3 a6a_shadow.py [data_dir] [plate_m2]      (plate default 750; under a minute; prints the figures quoted in submission section 7)
INPUT FILES in data_dir (default ./data). Each is a public extract, read as downloaded, nothing embedded here:
  cadastre_lots.json  NSW Spatial Services cadastre Lot layer (ArcGIS JSON: features[].attributes.planlabel/lotnumber, geometry.rings, lon/lat)
  osm_buildings.json  OpenStreetMap buildings (Overpass 'out geom' JSON, ODbL, (c) OSM contributors); tags height / building:levels

ASSUMPTIONS (none is exhibited by the Department): tower floor plate 30 x 25 m = 750 m2; existing 400 Glenmore Rd building 12 m (OSM has no
height); podium 4 x 3.34 m over the lot inset 10 m (south-east edge) and 5 m (south edge), other edges 0 m; tower 74.2 m; three tower
positions N / C / S (northernmost, central, southernmost fit inside the podium); ENV = whole podium raised to 74.2 m (upper bound, not a design).
Terrain from 10 m contours by inverse-distance weighting (about +/-3 m). 21 June, AEST, 10-minute steps. Other buildings, trees: ignored."""
import json, math, os, sys
import numpy as np

DATA = sys.argv[1] if len(sys.argv) > 1 and __name__ == "__main__" else "data"
ORIGIN = (-33.8799, 151.23225)                     # local equirectangular projection (x east, y north, metres)
KLAT, KLON = 110574.0, 111320.0 * math.cos(math.radians(ORIGIN[0]))
LAT, LON, DOY, TZ = -33.88, 151.2322, 172, 10      # site; 21 June = day 172; AEST
H_EXIST, STOREY, H_TOWER = 12.0, 3.34, 74.2           # ASSUMPTIONS: existing height, storey, tower height
PLATE_M2 = float(sys.argv[2]) if len(sys.argv) > 2 and __name__ == "__main__" else 750.0   # ASSUMPTION: tower floor plate, m2 (second argument)
PLATE = ((1.2 * PLATE_M2) ** 0.5, PLATE_M2 / (1.2 * PLATE_M2) ** 0.5)                      # rectangle with sides in the ratio 1.2 : 1 (30 x 25 m at 750 m2)
H_POD, SB_SE, SB_S, FLOOR_TO_FLOOR = 4 * STOREY, 10.0, 5.0, 3.0
ADG_LOSS_LINE = 20.0                               # ADG 3B-2 design criterion: no more than 20% loss of winter sun to neighbours


def load(f): return json.load(open(os.path.join(DATA, f)))
def xy(lat, lon): return ((lon - ORIGIN[1]) * KLON, (lat - ORIGIN[0]) * KLAT)
def area(p): return sum(p[i - 1][0] * p[i][1] - p[i][0] * p[i - 1][1] for i in range(len(p))) / 2.0
def ccw(p): return p if area(p) > 0 else p[::-1]


def centroid(p):
    a, cx, cy = area(p), 0.0, 0.0
    for i in range(len(p)):
        c = p[i - 1][0] * p[i][1] - p[i][0] * p[i - 1][1]; cx += (p[i - 1][0] + p[i][0]) * c; cy += (p[i - 1][1] + p[i][1]) * c
    return (cx / (6 * a), cy / (6 * a))


def contains(poly, pt):
    x, y, n = pt[0], pt[1], False
    for i in range(len(poly)):
        (x1, y1), (x2, y2) = poly[i - 1], poly[i]
        if (y1 > y) != (y2 > y) and x < (x2 - x1) * (y - y1) / (y2 - y1) + x1: n = not n
    return n


def osm_buildings():
    out = []
    for e in load("osm_buildings.json")["elements"]:
        g = e.get("geometry") if e["type"] == "way" else next((m.get("geometry") for m in e.get("members", []) if m.get("role") == "outer" and m.get("geometry")), None)
        if not g: continue

def sun(clock_h):
    """NOAA low-precision solar position: (altitude, azimuth from north) in radians."""
    g = 2 * math.pi / 365.0 * (DOY - 1 + (clock_h - TZ - 12) / 24.0)
    eq = 229.18 * (0.000075 + 0.001868 * math.cos(g) - 0.032077 * math.sin(g) - 0.014615 * math.cos(2 * g) - 0.040849 * math.sin(2 * g))
    dec = (0.006918 - 0.399912 * math.cos(g) + 0.070257 * math.sin(g) - 0.006758 * math.cos(2 * g) + 0.000907 * math.sin(2 * g)
           - 0.002697 * math.cos(3 * g) + 0.00148 * math.sin(3 * g))
    ha, la = math.radians((clock_h * 60 + eq + 4 * LON - 60 * TZ) / 4 - 180), math.radians(LAT)
    alt = math.asin(max(-1, min(1, math.sin(la) * math.sin(dec) + math.cos(la) * math.cos(dec) * math.cos(ha))))
    return alt, (math.atan2(math.sin(ha), math.cos(ha) * math.sin(la) - math.tan(dec) * math.cos(la)) + math.pi) % (2 * math.pi)


def pip_many(poly, pts):
    ins = np.zeros(len(pts), bool)
    for i in range(len(poly)):
        (x1, y1), (x2, y2) = poly[i - 1], poly[i]
        if y1 != y2: ins ^= ((y1 > pts[:, 1]) != (y2 > pts[:, 1])) & (pts[:, 0] < (x2 - x1) * (pts[:, 1] - y1) / (y2 - y1) + x1)
    return ins


def shadowed(prisms, pts, z, alt, az):
    """True where the ray from (pt, z) to the sun enters a vertical prism (footprint, top height): inside it, or within the parallelogram
    swept by a footprint edge along the shadow direction for (H - z)/tan(alt)."""
    if alt <= 0.0087: return np.ones(len(pts), bool)
    sd, res = (-math.sin(az), -math.cos(az)), np.zeros(len(pts), bool)
    for poly, H in prisms:
        ok, reach = H > z, np.maximum(H - z, 0) / math.tan(alt)
        hit = pip_many(poly, pts) & ok
        for i in range(len(poly)):
            a = np.array(poly[i - 1]); e = np.array(poly[i]) - a; det = e[0] * sd[1] - sd[0] * e[1]
            if abs(det) < 1e-9: continue
            d = pts - a; u = (d[:, 0] * sd[1] - sd[0] * d[:, 1]) / det; t = (e[0] * d[:, 1] - e[1] * d[:, 0]) / det
            hit |= (u >= 0) & (u <= 1) & (t >= 0) & (t <= reach) & ok
        res |= hit
    return res


def clip(poly, a, b, d):
    """Keep the part of CCW poly left of directed line a->b moved d metres inward."""
    L = math.hypot(b[0] - a[0], b[1] - a[1]); nx, ny = -(b[1] - a[1]) / L, (b[0] - a[0]) / L
    s = lambda p: (p[0] - a[0] - nx * d) * nx + (p[1] - a[1] - ny * d) * ny
    out = []
    for i in range(len(poly)):
        p, q = poly[i - 1], poly[i]; sp, sq = s(p), s(q); c = None
        if (sq >= 0) != (sp >= 0): t = sp / (sp - sq); c = (p[0] + t * (q[0] - p[0]), p[1] + t * (q[1] - p[1]))
        if sq >= 0 and c and sp < 0: out.append(c)
        if sq >= 0: out.append(q)
        elif sp >= 0 and c: out.append(c)
    return out


def podium(lot):

def towers(pod):

def seg_dist(p, a, b):
```

```python
"""a6b_traffic_routes.py - tower trips, Paddington route test and planning-control arithmetic for 400 Glenmore Road.
Python 3 + numpy only. Imports helpers from a6a_shadow.py.
Run:  python3 a6b_traffic_routes.py [raw_data_dir]      (a few seconds)
INPUT FILES (public extracts, read as downloaded; nothing embedded here). In raw_data_dir (default ./data):
  osm_roads_wide.json, osm_roads_local.json  OpenStreetMap highway ways (Overpass 'out geom' JSON, ODbL): tags highway/name/oneway/maxspeed/access
  osm_junction_nodes.json                    OpenStreetMap nodes tagged highway=traffic_signals (lat, lon, tags)
  osm_paddington_boundary.json               OpenStreetMap suburb boundary relation (Paddington; outer-way members with geometry)
  osm_buildings_paddington.json              OpenStreetMap buildings in the suburb (tags building, building:levels), a dwelling-count proxy
  derived/census_g36_dwellings.csv (a folder next to the extracts)        ABS Census 2021 table G36 (dwelling structure), SAL 'Paddington (NSW)': columns region,structure,sum,value
                                             (row 'Total private dwellings', Dwellings = 6,435). LEP layers (Planning Portal) give the controls below.
ASSUMPTIONS: peak-hour vehicle trips per dwelling 0.10 / 0.17 / 0.24 (own derivation from TfNSW survey charts x an assumed 10% peak share;
the TfNSW guide itself was not read); net new homes 20 / 90 / 125 (own arithmetic from FSR, lot area and 52 existing units). Routes are STATIC: free-flow speed from maxspeed else a
default by road class, 15 s per signal, no turn restrictions, no congestion. Destinations are approximate points snapped to arterial nodes.
Dwelling weights = footprint x levels (2; 3 for apartments) of residential-type buildings, scaled to the Census total. Origins = points every
40 m (first at 20 m on each segment) on residential-type streets inside the Paddington boundary. 'Uses the link' = route passes OSM ways
173836380 / 222254194 (Glenmore Road to New South Head Road)."""
import csv, heapq, math, os, sys
import numpy as np
import a6a_shadow as A
from a6a_shadow import xy, area, centroid, contains

if len(sys.argv) > 1: A.DATA = sys.argv[1]
LINK = {173836380, 222254194}
DRIVE = {"motorway": 80, "motorway_link": 60, "trunk": 70, "trunk_link": 50, "primary": 60, "primary_link": 40, "secondary": 50, "secondary_link": 40,
         "tertiary": 50, "tertiary_link": 40, "unclassified": 50, "residential": 50, "living_street": 20}      # default km/h by class
MAJOR = {"motorway", "trunk", "primary", "secondary", "motorway_link", "trunk_link", "primary_link", "secondary_link"}
RES_LIKE = {"residential", "living_street", "unclassified", "tertiary"}
RES_BLDG = {"house", "terrace", "apartments", "residential", "detached", "semidetached_house", "yes", "dormitory", "townhouse"}
DEST = {"Town Hall": (-33.8732, 151.2069), "Airport": (-33.9340, 151.1835), "Olympic Park": (-33.8470, 151.0690), "Parramatta": (-33.8150, 151.0030)}
RATES, HOMES = (0.10, 0.17, 0.24), (20, 90, 125)
key = lambda p: (round(p["lat"], 7), round(p["lon"], 7))


def graph():
    ways = {}
    for f in ("osm_roads_wide.json", "osm_roads_local.json"): ways.update({e["id"]: e for e in A.load(f)["elements"]})
    sig = {(round(n["lat"], 7), round(n["lon"], 7)) for n in A.load("osm_junction_nodes.json")["elements"] if n.get("tags", {}).get("highway") == "traffic_signals"}
    idx, pts, radj = {}, [], []
    def nid(k):
        if k not in idx: idx[k] = len(pts); pts.append(xy(*k)); radj.append([])
        return idx[k]
    for wid, e in ways.items():
        t = e.get("tags", {}); hw = t.get("highway")
        if hw not in DRIVE or not e.get("geometry") or "no" in (t.get("access"), t.get("motor_vehicle"), t.get("vehicle")) or "private" in (t.get("access"), t.get("motor_vehicle"), t.get("vehicle")): continue
        o = t.get("oneway", "")
        ow = 1 if o in ("yes", "true", "1") else -1 if o == "-1" else 0 if o == "no" else 1 if (t.get("junction") in ("roundabout", "circular") or hw.startswith("motorway")) else 0
        try: v = float(t.get("maxspeed", "").split()[0]); v = v if 5 <= v <= 130 else DRIVE[hw]
        except (ValueError, IndexError): v = DRIVE[hw]
        ks = [key(p) for p in e["geometry"] ]
        for a, b in zip(ks[:-1], ks[1:]):
            if a == b: continue
            u, w = nid(a), nid(b); L = math.dist(pts[u], pts[w])
            for p, q, kq, ok in ((u, w, b, ow >= 0), (w, u, a, ow <= 0)):
                if ok: radj[q].append((p, L, L / (v / 3.6) + (15.0 if kq in sig else 0.0), wid))      # edge p->q stored on q: (from, metres, seconds, way)
    return ways, np.array(pts), radj


def tree(radj, dest, ci):
    """Dijkstra towards dest on the reversed graph (ci 2 = seconds, 1 = metres): dist, next node, next way, settled order."""
    n = len(radj); dist = [math.inf] * n; nxt = [-1] * n; nxw = [None] * n; order = []; dist[dest] = 0.0; pq = [(0.0, dest)]; done = [False] * n
    while pq:
        d, u = heapq.heappop(pq)
        if done[u]: continue
        done[u] = True; order.append(u)
        for e in radj[u]:
            if d + e[ci] < dist[e[0] ]: dist[e[0] ] = d + e[ci]; nxt[e[0] ] = u; nxw[e[0] ] = e[3]; heapq.heappush(pq, (dist[e[0] ], e[0]))
    return dist, nxt, nxw, order


def ring_of_boundary():
    segs = [ [xy(p["lat"], p["lon"]) for p in m["geometry"] ] for m in A.load("osm_paddington_boundary.json")["elements"][0]["members"] if m["type"] == "way" and m["role"] == "outer" and m.get("geometry")]
    ring = segs.pop(0)
    while segs:
        for i, s in enumerate(segs):
            if math.dist(s[0], ring[-1]) < .5: ring += s[1:]
            elif math.dist(s[-1], ring[-1]) < .5: ring += s[::-1][1:]
            elif math.dist(s[-1], ring[0]) < .5: ring = s[:-1] + ring
            elif math.dist(s[0], ring[0]) < .5: ring = s[::-1][:-1] + ring
            else: continue
            segs.pop(i); break
        else: raise SystemExit("boundary ring did not close")
    return ring


def streets_south_of_nsh():
    """Named public streets (not service/links/driveways, at least 25 m long) leaving the SOUTH side of New South Head Road between Neild Avenue and Ocean Avenue."""
    R = list(A.load("osm_roads_local.json")["elements"])
    nsh = {key(p) for e in R if e["tags"].get("name") == "New South Head Road" for p in e["geometry"]}
    ends = lambda nm: [xy(*key(p))[0] for e in R if e["tags"].get("name") == nm for p in e["geometry"] if key(p) in nsh]
    x0, x1 = min(ends("Neild Avenue")), max(ends("Ocean Avenue")); found = set()
    for e in R:
        t = e["tags"]
        if t.get("highway") not in RES_LIKE | {"tertiary_link"} or not t.get("name") or t["name"] in ("Neild Avenue", "Ocean Avenue"): continue
        L = sum(math.dist(xy(*key(a)), xy(*key(b))) for a, b in zip(e["geometry"][:-1], e["geometry"][1:]))
        for p in e["geometry"]:
            x, y = xy(*key(p))
            if key(p) in nsh and x0 - 20 <= x <= x1 + 20 and L >= 25 and xy(*key(max(e["geometry"], key=lambda q: math.dist(xy(*key(q)), (x, y)))))[1] < y: found.add(t["name"])
    return sorted(found)


def main():
    print("(C) Tower peak-hour trips = net new homes x trips per home (two-way):")
    for h, r in zip(HOMES, RATES): print(f"    {h:3d} homes x {r:.2f} = {h * r:5.1f}/h")
    print(f"(D) Streets leaving the south side of New South Head Road, Neild Ave to Ocean Ave: {streets_south_of_nsh()}")
    total = next(int(r["value"]) for r in csv.DictReader(open(os.path.join(A.DATA, "..", "derived", "census_g36_dwellings.csv")))
                 if r["region"] == "Paddington (NSW)" and r["structure"] == "Total private dwellings" and r["sum"] == "Dwellings")
    ways, pts, radj = graph(); ring = ring_of_boundary()
    nearest = lambda x, y, m=None: int(np.where(m if m is not None else True, np.hypot(pts[:, 0] - x, pts[:, 1] - y), 1e18).argmin())
    major = np.zeros(len(pts), bool)
    for q in range(len(radj)):
        for e in radj[q]:
            if ways[e[3] ]["tags"].get("highway") in MAJOR: major[e[0] ] = True      # node has an outgoing arterial edge
    org = []
    for e in ways.values():
        if e["tags"].get("highway") in RES_LIKE and e.get("geometry"):
            g = [xy(p["lat"], p["lon"]) for p in e["geometry"] ]
            for a, b in zip(g[:-1], g[1:]):
                L = math.dist(a, b); s = 20.0
                while s < L:
                    q = (a[0] + (b[0] - a[0]) * s / L, a[1] + (b[1] - a[1]) * s / L); s += 40.0
                    if contains(ring, q): org.append(q)
    oa = np.array(org); w = np.zeros(len(org))
    for e in A.load("osm_buildings_paddington.json")["elements"]:
        t = e.get("tags", {})
        if e["type"] != "way" or not e.get("geometry") or t.get("building") not in RES_BLDG: continue
        p = [xy(q["lat"], q["lon"]) for q in e["geometry"] ]; c = centroid(p)
        if contains(ring, c):
            try: lv = float(t.get("building:levels", 3 if t["building"] == "apartments" else 2))
            except ValueError: lv = 2
            w[int(np.hypot(oa[:, 0] - c[0], oa[:, 1] - c[1]).argmin())] += abs(area(p)) * lv
    dw = w / w.sum() * total; on = [nearest(*o) for o in org]
    print(f"(D) Route test: {len(org)} origins, {total:,} dwellings (Census 2021, scaled by footprint x levels). Share whose route uses the Glenmore Rd / NSH Rd link:")
    for name, (la, lo) in DEST.items():
        for metric, ci in (("fastest", 2), ("shortest", 1)):
            dist, nxt, nxw, order = tree(radj, nearest(*xy(la, lo), major), ci); uses = {}
            for u in order: uses[u] = nxt[u] != -1 and (nxw[u] in LINK or uses[nxt[u] ])
            u_ = np.array([uses.get(i, False) for i in on]); ok = np.array([dist[i] < 1e17 for i in on])
            print(f"    {name:12} {metric:8} {100 * (dw * u_ * ok).sum() / (dw * ok).sum():4.1f}%  ({(dw * u_ * ok).sum():.0f} dwellings)")
    print("(E) Arithmetic (LEP height and FSR now 13.5 m and 0.75:1; proposed 74.2 m and 2.2:1; lot 6,238 m2; homes of 100 to 130 m2; two storeys of 7 m):")
    extra = (2.2 - 0.75) * 6238
    print(f"    height {74.2 / 13.5:.2f}x; FSR {2.2 / 0.75:.2f}x; extra floor area {extra:,.0f} m2; homes {extra / 130:.0f} to {extra / 100:.0f}; versus 7 m building {74.2 / 7:.1f}x")


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
```
